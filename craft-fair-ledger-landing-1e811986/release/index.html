A hypothetical worked example

10 sales cover these event costs.

This example explains the arithmetic only; it is not a preview of the paid product or a customer result.

Imagine paying an $80 stall fee and $20 for travel. You sell each item for $15, with a $5 variable cost per item sold.

  1. Add the event costs

    $80 stall fee + $20 travel = $100 in event costs.

  2. Find the contribution per item

    $15 selling price − $5 variable cost = $10 contribution per item. This is the amount left from each sale to put toward the event costs.

  3. Work out the sales needed

    $100 event costs ÷ $10 contribution = 10 sales to cover those event costs.

At 10 sales: $150 in sales revenue − $50 in variable costs − $100 in event costs = $0 left over.

Your working time and other expenses still count.

This example excludes compensation for your working time and any other expenses. Ten sales only cover the $80 stall fee, $20 travel and the $5 variable cost for each item sold. It does not mean you have paid yourself or covered every cost of running your business.
